02
The problem

Graffiti works because of where it is.

You have to go to the wall. That's the whole point — and it's exactly what the internet strips out. Post it online and it becomes an image of a place instead of the place.

Meanwhile the real thing gets painted over, and the people who paint it get fined.

We wanted a version where your tag stays put, costs nobody a wall, and someone on the other side of the world can still stand in front of it.

One palette file drives the artwork, the map, the markers and the charts.

05
Technical core — the part that's hard

Placing something is easy. Agreeing on where it is is the hard part.

1 · Marker frame

A printed marker gives every device the same origin. We send a markerTransform on the wire, so "here" means the same thing on your phone and mine — indoors, where GPS is useless.

2 · World-map chaining

Each visitor's ARWorldMap merges into the venue's map, versioned with lineage. The spot gets easier to find the more people scan it. A ghosted guide shows you where to stand to relocalize.

3 · Depth & occlusion

The decal wraps to the LiDAR depth map instead of sitting on a flat quad, and real geometry occludes it. Walk behind a pillar and the paint goes behind the pillar.

Wall placement, relocalized from the shared frame.

06
Craft — 20% of the score, and the whole Reve track

We made it behave like aerosol, not like a sticker.

  • Feathered noise edges — overspray, not a crisp alpha cut
  • Colour pulled toward the wall so pigment sits in the surface
  • Grazing-incidence thinning — paint thins as the wall turns away from you
  • Multi-coat variation so no two passes look identical
  • Sobel wall-grain normal map — brick and concrete texture read through the paint

Applied to remote pieces too, so someone else's tag looks painted on your wall, not pasted over your camera. Plus an ARCoachingOverlay so a stranger never sees a frozen screen.

07
A stranger sees it — no install, just a link

The map is the public half of the project.

Hand-authored basemap over free OpenFreeMap tiles — no stock style, no map API key. Three zoom tiers: density glow → pins tinted with each piece's own dominant colour → the framed artwork itself.

Every piece carries the photo of the surface it was painted on, plus an async Replicate upscale and weathering pass.

09
Lasting value

A community garden, not a personal diary.

Every visitor who scans the venue makes the world map thicker, so the next person relocalizes faster. The wall genuinely gets better the more people show up — that's a property of the system, not a promise.

  • Shared coordinate frame means a spot is a place, not one person's session
  • Area galleries — a surface fills up and you scroll it
  • The map is public and permanent; the paint costs nobody a wall

10
Three tracks, one build

What we're claiming, and why.

Main

One moment of input

Under 60 seconds, zero explanation. The transformation is real and named: a finger drawing becomes a depth-wrapped, aerosol-shaded decal locked to a shared coordinate frame. Public, live, and better with more people.

Best Use of Reve

A visual identity, not a filter

Reve is the design backbone — the house style for the artwork. Every colour in both apps comes from one file, hand-tuned, and hand-edited output only. Nothing here is a default AI aesthetic.

TECHNATION

Real, current, explorable

Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY → SSE. Surface classification, geography and client breakdown, updating live. Seeded rows are labelled as seeded.

26 hours, two people, two apps, one API contract. Self-hosted: Docker, TLS, deployed from main on push.

11
What's next

Walls, crews, and time.

  • Persistent shared anchors beyond a session — Cloud Anchors or our own world-map store, so a spot survives without the printed marker
  • Walls and crews — a surface that fills up, and the people who claim it
  • Time-lapse — scrub a wall's history and watch it get painted over
  • Android via WebXR, so the barrier is a link rather than an install
  • Community moderation — this is public art pinned to real addresses, and the neighbourhood should get a say
